Lola’s story

Lola Cherie
Zimmer

Born September 26, 2018 · Arizona

“She was tough and silly all at once. She was loved and changed so many lives.”

Alyssa Aguilar, Lola’s mom

Lola Cherie Zimmer was born in Arizona to Alyssa and Drew. A big sister to Phoebe, she loved nature, animals, dresses, and time with her family. Fishing, hiking, and trips to the movies were among the things they loved doing together. Her mom remembers her as strong-willed, determined, and wonderfully silly.

Shortly after her sixth birthday, Lola was diagnosed with high-risk B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Through months of treatment, her family held on to hope. Her life was cut short by a fungal infection while she was severely immunocompromised.

In her memory, her family created Lola’s Butterfly Effect Foundation. Alyssa saw how much joy simple toys and games brought Lola during treatment. That experience lives at the heart of the foundation’s mission: uplifting warriors through the power of play and imagination.

Read more of Lola’s story

In the days before her diagnosis, Lola’s family noticed changes: unusual tiredness, less interest in her favorite activities, headaches, and a nosebleed that lasted too long. On Halloween night, as they prepared to go trick-or-treating, Lola became distressed and fell to the floor. Her parents took her to the emergency room, where they learned she had leukemia. She was airlifted to Phoenix Children’s Hospital.

The months that followed brought chemotherapy, hospital stays, infections, procedures, and difficult days. Alyssa remembers Lola remaining optimistic and smiling. When Lola did not reach remission after induction, the first phase of chemotherapy, her family remained hopeful that the next phase, consolidation, would bring different news.

After an admission that included a week in isolation, Lola returned home. She had completed her final chemotherapy treatment of the consolidation phase and was due for another bone marrow test. Less than 24 hours after discharge, her family was back at the hospital.

Alyssa describes a rapidly progressing fungal infection that sent Lola into septic shock. Her family witnessed resuscitation efforts and watched their daughter fight for her life. Lola needed life support, and her parents were eventually told she would not survive.

Through their grief, the family created a foundation in Lola’s memory. Its projects include slime kits, arts and crafts, and stuffed animals donated to a local children’s hospital—small opportunities for play, imagination, and comfort, inspired by what had brought Lola joy.

In Alyssa’s words: “She was our first born daughter, she made me a mother and I miss her everyday.”

The power of play.

At Lola’s Butterfly Effect Foundation, our mission is to uplift warriors through the power of play and imagination.

Based in Salem, Oregon, our foundation holds childhood cancer, grief, child loss, and big love at the heart of its purpose.
